# Word-card prompt: linguistic standards and storage adaptation

Research date: **2026-10-08**. Input: the supplied Spanish “Generador de tarjetas Anki de alemán (ES → DE)” prompt. This is a review of its rules and selected examples, **not a verification of every catalog record**. The findings below are recommendations for an audit; they do not describe deployed changes.

## What can be adopted

Keep one card per **construction and intended meaning**, verified noun articles/plurals, visible object case, construction-specific auxiliaries, idiomatic German examples with Spanish translations, and separate reflexive constructions where the meaning or pronoun case changes. The five allowed display annotations are a product convention. They do not prevent retaining grammatical categories and noun gender as structured catalog metadata.

Use Duden's entry and inflection table for a noun's article/plural and a verb's forms. Duden explains that its grammar section gives noun article, singular genitive and nominative plural, and verb perfect auxiliary; some details are attached to a particular meaning. Use an IDS valency **sense entry**, not just the lemma, to check complements and their cases. [Duden: grammatical information](https://www.duden.de/hilfe/grammatik), [IDS: dictionary of verb valency](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/verbs/search).

## Verified rules and limitations

| Area | Standard for the audit | Checked evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noun gender/plural | Verify the intended sense; do not guess from endings or from a plural article. `Die` in a nominative plural card does not identify a feminine singular lexeme. | `Der Chili` → `Die Chilis`; `Der Kiefer` (jaw) → `Die Kiefer`; `Die Kiefer` (tree) → `Die Kiefern`. The wood sense of `Kiefer` has no plural. [Chili](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Chili), [Kiefer: jaw](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Kiefer_Knochen), [Kiefer: tree/wood](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Kiefer_Baum). |
| Homographs | Separate verified meanings and construction identities. Gender is useful but is not a universal sense identifier across regional varieties. | `Der Gehalt` (content/substance) has `Die Gehalte`; salary has `Die Gehälter`, with `das` generally and `der` in Austrian usage. [Gehalt: substance](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Gehalt_Substanz), [Gehalt: salary](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Gehalt_Lohn). |
| Verb valency | A two-way preposition's case in a verb complement is lexical; the spatial movement/location shortcut is insufficient. Keep all complements needed to identify the meaning. | Participation uses `an + Dativ`; IDS also records rare `bei + Dativ` for that sense. `einer Krankheit vorbeugen` uses a dative complement. These support the prompt's article-bearing examples. [IDS: teilnehmen, sense 1](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/verbs/view/400947/1), [Duden: vorbeugen](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/vorbeugen). |
| Reflexive case | Check the complete construction. The `mich/mir` and `dich/dir` forms make case visible; `sich/uns/euch` do not. Another accusative object is a useful clue, not proof by itself. | IDS distinguishes `Ich wasche mich` from `Ich wasche mir … die Hände`, and identifies imagining as dative `sich vorstellen`. [IDS: reflexive complements](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/progr%40mm/6736), [IDS: sich vorstellen, sense 1](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/verbs/view/401123/1). |
| Auxiliary | Verify auxiliary by exact sense and construction. Do not normalize every lemma to one auxiliary or every occurrence of `sich` to `hat`. | Movement `fahren` takes `sein`; steering a vehicle takes `haben`; some racing/distance constructions admit both. `passieren` meaning “happen” takes `sein`, while passing a border or straining food takes `haben`. [Duden: fahren](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/fahren), [Duden: passieren](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/passieren). |
| Imperative | Store an idiomatic, attested form only where the intended construction supports it. Verify irregular stems; do not generate forms by dropping infinitive endings. | IDS covers `e → i/ie` changes, the exception `werde`, and variable final `-e`. Core modal verbs are not used in the imperative. [IDS: imperative forms](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/1275), [IDS: verb stems](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/2769), [IDS: modal verbs](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/1548). |
| Current senses and register | Distinguish existence, current frequency, register and learning usefulness. Do not equate “formal” with every elevated, literary, regional or technical expression. | Duden labels `die Kunde` meaning “news” elevated and becoming obsolete, supporting its exclusion from the prompt's common-vocabulary selection. An exclusion is not a claim that the word never exists. [Duden: Kunde, news](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Kunde_Nachricht). |
| Separable families | Verify each member, its separation, sense and useful frequency independently. Never multiply a root by an arbitrary prefix list. | The prompt's claim that `wegfangen` does not exist is false: Duden lists it as colloquial, with low frequency. It can be excluded on usefulness/frequency grounds, but not declared nonexistent. [Duden: wegfangen](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/wegfangen). |

The noun examples above are selected checks, not a closed vocabulary list. A general plural line must not override a sense-level “without plural” restriction; `Kiefer` as wood illustrates why.

## Corrections and ambiguities in the supplied prompt

1. **“Reflexive verbs always use `hat`” needs construction-specific exceptions.** IDS explicitly allows `sein` in reciprocal dative constructions when the non-reciprocal verb also takes it, including `sich begegnen` and `sich entgegengehen`. The prompt groups reciprocal `sich` under its broader rules, so a blanket rewrite would be incorrect. Preserve dative case in audit metadata even if the display convention suppresses a reflexive label for reciprocal cards. [IDS: haben oder sein?](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/kontrastive-grammatik/4160). The copular adjective construction `sich einer Gefahr bewusst sein` also keeps **sein**: it combines a dative reflexive with genitive content rather than a lexical reflexive verb requiring **haben**. [Duden: bewusst](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/bewusst), [IDS: dative and genitive complements](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/kontrastive-grammatik/4365).

2. **Person/number and imperative availability are separate questions.** For the mutual-acquaintance reading, the sample `hat sich kennengelernt` is incompatible with its intended plural participants. Prefer complete `wir haben uns kennengelernt` and `wir lernten uns kennen`; a useful plural imperative is `lernt euch kennen!`, an application of the documented plural imperative. Collective singular subjects can also express a reciprocal relation; IDS gives `die Prominenz` with `sich treffen`. Do not infer “no imperative” solely from reciprocity, or classify every reciprocal construction as grammatically plural-only. [Duden: kennenlernen](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/kennenlernen), [Duden: its conjugation](https://www.duden.de/konjugation/kennenlernen), [IDS: sich treffen, reciprocal note](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/verbs/view/400955/1).

3. **`es lohnt sich` is a useful phrase, not proof that `es` is obligatory.** Duden also documents lexical subjects such as `der Aufwand` and `das Geschäft`. The prompt itself uses `Die Reise hat sich … gelohnt` as an example. A card may intentionally teach `es lohnt sich`, but its metadata must allow the actual subject pattern of the chosen sense. [Duden: lohnen](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/lohnen).

4. **Modal perfect forms require construction checks.** `hat gekonnt` is suitable for an independent use of `können`; with another governed infinitive the standard pattern uses an Ersatzinfinitiv, e.g. `hat kommen können`. A generated collocation must not inherit the standalone form blindly. [IDS: Ersatzinfinitiv](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/1615).

5. **The negative imperative for `sich Sorgen machen` is a presentation choice, not an established “only existing form” rule.** `mach dir keine Sorgen!` is useful. This research did not establish that positive imperatives are impossible. Require construction evidence before recording that stronger assertion. Similarly, “exactly three lines” must be read with the prompt's explicit exceptions for missing imperatives, dative annotation and idioms. [Duden: machen, including sich Sorgen machen](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/machen), [IDS: imperative use](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/4561).

6. **Some formatting priorities need explicit resolution.** Multi-complement numbering in section 4 is an internal checking procedure; the model cards do not display `(1)/(2)` and the annotation list forbids adding arbitrary labels. Treat idiomatic `Redewendung` notes as taking precedence over verb conjugation notes, as the model idiom demonstrates. Uppercasing an initial article and forcing complement-first Spanish are user-defined card conventions, not lexical corrections. Do not make those transformations alter case or accepted-answer semantics.

7. **Import order does not guarantee study order.** Anki has separate insertion, gather and sort controls. The “three unrelated cards between family members” rule describes the generated list; it cannot certify spacing in an app queue without checking that queue's actual ordering behavior. [Anki: deck options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/deck-options).

## Plain-text storage adaptation

The user-facing four-field Anki format is an **export representation**, not a database schema. For native generation, keep the API's structured JSON fields. Write plain Unicode text with real `\n` line breaks in `notes`; write separate strings in `examples`. Do not store `<br>`, HTML, TSV records or serialized arrays as display text. Empty notes are `""`; absent examples are `[]` where the contract requires an array.

Existing source confirms the relevant contract: `Word.notes?: string`, `Word.examples: string[]`, structured `Word.forms`, and explicit `WordRelation.main/translated` references in [words.types.ts](../src/features/words/words.types.ts). The archived Anki adapter already converts HTML line breaks into newlines and splits example lines; it also separates German and Spanish example text. See [scripts/lib/anki.ts](../scripts/lib/anki.ts). Adapt only at the boundary and preserve paired example ordering, umlauts, `ß`, punctuation and the five permitted display annotations.

A display rule forbidding gender/category labels does **not** mean deleting `forms.gender` or `gramaticalCategories`. A front such as `Die Häuser` is a plural-card identity; treating its `Die` as singular gender would be wrong. Do not merge `Der Kiefer` and `Die Kiefer`, distinct reflexive constructions, or different meanings merely because normalized text is similar. Preserve catalog IDs, relation IDs and directional card identities. Existing scheduler state, progress, mistakes, ownership and archived Anki source fields are outside a vocabulary-content correction.

## Verification workflow

1. Freeze a dated, read-only snapshot and a scope manifest. Keep the original values and their checksum beside each proposed change. Separate source/format normalization from semantic corrections.
2. For every noun correction record lemma, intended meaning, article, plural restrictions, source URL and check date. For every verb record sense, complements/cases, reflexive/reciprocal case, auxiliary, principal parts and actual imperative availability. A lemma-level dictionary hit alone does not verify all these properties.
3. Use Duden and IDS for lexical/morphosyntactic checks. Use a dated contemporary corpus query, such as DWDS or IDS DeReKo, to support claims about sense frequency. Inspect concordances rather than trusting raw string counts, which can mix meanings. **DWDS was inaccessible to this research tool because of robots restrictions; no DWDS frequency audit or catalog-wide verification is claimed.**
4. Classify each record as verified unchanged, verified correction, unresolved, or excluded by selection policy. Leave unresolved semantics unchanged and report the missing evidence. An empty form is not automatically proof that the form does not exist.
5. Validate format mechanically: plain text, annotation placement, preserved empty fields, example pairing and exact object order in the intentionally literal Spanish cue. Use hand-checked fixtures for dative/accusative reflexives, reciprocal `sein`, modal perfect, homographs, plural-only nouns and missing imperatives.
6. Produce a reviewable diff, reversible migration manifest and rollback data. Apply only approved exact records; refuse stale values. Re-audit the changed records after application. Verify equivalent web/native rendering and answer behavior before claiming parity. A local dry run proves the migration machinery, not German correctness or production completion.

Completeness requires a per-record evidence ledger for the chosen catalog scope. These selected rule checks support that process and cannot replace it.
